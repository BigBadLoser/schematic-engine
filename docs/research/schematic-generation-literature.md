# Schematic generation from netlists: literature and tools survey

Date 2026-10-12. Web search and page fetches only. Several classics (May/Szymanski, Lageweg, Brandenburg, Lienig/Scheible, Nlview internals) were not found online or not readable; marked "unverified". Patents and abstracts were read, not full papers.

## Sources
1. **US 7,917,877 "System and method for circuit schematic generation"** (Cadence, 2011) https://patents.google.com/patent/US7917877 . Netlist to placed and routed OpenAccess. Devices grouped: MOS "tree" instances, two-terminal series/parallel/RC clusters, black boxes. A template library (inverter, mirror, diff pair) fixes quadrant and orientation. Grid placement: columns seeded from a BFS tree and grown along connections; row = BFS height from the power net down to ground (power top, ground bottom). Two-terminal parts are "attachments" above/below the device they touch, in its column. Global routing prefers readability over wirelength.
2. **US 8,504,953 "Schematic generation visualization aid for netlists comprising analog circuits"** https://patents.google.com/patent/US8504953 . Deterministic (components sorted by name). Recursive grouping of recognised sub-circuits that replace their members. DFS assigns groups to columns; Kernighan-Lin min-cut then simplex sets rows to minimise crossings and length. Inside a group: top to bottom by current flow. Pin order flipped when drain is on power or source on ground. Between groups: left-edge channel routing. No benchmarks.
3. **US 5,187,784 "IC placement using netlist and predetermined ordering constraints"** https://patents.google.com/patent/US5187784 . Min-cut partitioning where "A below/left of B" ordering constraints add to the cut cost. Left-to-right signal flow is encoded as horizontal ordering constraints. Goals: close, few crossings, few bends.
4. **Hsu and Lin, "Automatic Analog Schematic Diagram Generation based on Building Block Classification and Reinforcement Learning", MLCAD 2022** https://unpaywall.org/10.1145%2F3551901.3556486 (abstract only). Classifies building blocks, then RL placement; reports higher block-compliance and fewer bends/crossings than prior tools. Needs a large sub-circuit library.
5. **Arsintescu, "A method for analog circuits visualization", ICCD 1996; Lee and McNamee, aesthetic routing for transistor schematics, ICCAD 1992** (known only via citations in 4 and 6). Symmetry-based placement; crossing-minimising routing. Later authors say these "failed to generalise across circuit types".
6. **EEschematic (LLM agent), arXiv 2510.17002, 2025** https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17002v1 . LLM identifies sub-structures and proposes placement with flips/rotation for symmetry; wires by net-name grouping; wiring priority VDD, GND, gate, drain, source, bulk, then I/O, R, C. Judged on symmetry, alignment, compactness, clarity (no hard numbers read).
7. **Weave, arXiv 2607.03835, 2026** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03835 . SPICE to LTspice .asc via elkjs layered (Sugiyama) layout. Nets classified ground/rail/signal. Special placement patterns for feedback loops, divider legs and shunts, kept outside the main graph. Orthogonal grid routing. Round-trip connectivity check with a safe-mode fallback ladder. 100% on 117 circuits; 88.4% of 3,460 ADI demos; fails on dense multi-pin power modules. Authors admit aesthetics are traded for exactness. Closest to our stack (ELK), and shows plain ELK layered is not enough alone.
8. **US 6,980,211 "Automatic schematic diagram generation using topology information"** https://patents.google.com/patent/US6980211 (digital/EDA). Placement templates chosen per selected component: central, fan-in/fan-out (tiers by DFS to one side), **path (selected components in one row, others hang around it)**, **bus (parts on both sides of a selected net)**. Router groups nets with identical driver+load into classes and routes each class as one bundle.
9. **Eclipse ELK Layered** https://eclipse.dev/elk/reference/groups/org-eclipse-elk-port.html . Sugiyama with port constraints (FIXED_SIDE/ORDER/POS), orthogonal routing; needs directed input.
10. **Nlview (Concept Engineering)**: only marketing text found (gate/RTL/block engines, optional transistor-level engine, incremental layout, user control). No public rules. Unverified: Cadence/Mentor/LTspice/Qucs importer conventions; May/Szymanski; Lageweg; Brandenburg; Lienig/Scheible.

## Common model across the analog systems
- Columns = signal flow (left to right) or connectivity depth; rows = current flow (supply top, ground bottom).
- Recognised sub-circuit/template groups are placed first as rigid units, then arranged.
- Two-terminal parts are "attachments" to the device they touch, in the same column (above/below), not independent nodes.
- Determinism (sorted inputs) is stated as a feature.
- Routing: channel/left-edge or readability-biased global routing; none uses named labels as a first-class tool (Weave tags nets only for verification).

## Lessons for our engine (ranked)
1. **Path and bus templates as first-class placement units (src 8, 1).** Extract the main path (source to load, longest/most-connected chain) and pin it to one row as a rigid chain; rungs/shunts hang as attachments in the part's column. Maps to: after the ELK floorplan, the relation-placement pass emits row constraints (same y, increasing x) for the path; refinement may not violate them (hard constraint, not a cost term). Fixes problems 1 and 2. A ladder is a bus template: trunk net along a row, rungs as attachments above/below, second trunk as the other row.
2. **Column = depth, row = current flow, solved separately (src 2, 5).** Longest-path depth along signal direction gives x (monotone: no path edge points backward); crossing/length minimisation (KL or ILP) gives y within a column. Maps to ELK layered with direction RIGHT and fixed port sides, then refinement moves parts only in y within a column unless a cost-gain test passes. Addresses doubling back (1).
3. **Attachments follow their anchor (src 1, 7).** Every shunt/bypass/feedback two-terminal gets an anchor part and a side (above/below) and moves with it; refinement moves anchor plus attachments together. Fixes clusters (2) and density (4).
4. **Net classes for wire-vs-label (src 8, 7).** Classify nets: ground/rail (symbol or flag), local (few pins, short bounding box: wire), long or fan-out beyond the row (label). Decide after placement using routed length and crossings, then re-route label-converted nets away. Maps to the labels stage after libavoid. Addresses (3).
5. **Orientation chosen from rail connectivity before refinement (src 2, 6).** Pins on supply face up, ground down; series parts on the path row horizontal, shunts vertical. Matches our measured 73-95% frequencies; do it as a selection step, not a post-hoc cost. Addresses (5).
6. **Determinism plus verify-and-fall-back ladder (src 2, 7).** Sort inputs; round-trip check connectivity; fall back to a simpler layout on failure. Maps to the existing gate.

## Contradictions and flags
- Literature puts supply on top and ground at bottom by BFS rows; with a main path pinned to one row this can conflict with ELK layered's depth ordering when the path is not the longest chain. Choose the path first.
- Most systems run placement then routing strictly in sequence; our greedy refinement with a cost is unusual and may reintroduce wander unless hard row constraints (lesson 1) are added.
- Weave shows ELK layered alone favours connectivity over aesthetics, so our extra relation pass is justified.
- Sources are mostly patents and abstracts; quantitative quality data for rows or density is essentially absent, so readability thresholds must come from our own corpus measurements.
